Takku and Granola

Both record your calls without a bot and turn them into notes. The difference is where your meetings go.

Granola is a polished cloud service with apps for every device. Takku is a free Mac app that keeps the audio on your Mac and writes into your Obsidian vault. What follows about Granola comes from its own pages, read in October 2026.

Side by side

Takku Granola
Recording No bot: your microphone and what your Mac plays No bot: your device’s audio
Transcription On your Mac, with Whisper or Parakeet By cloud providers such as Deepgram and Assembly
Audio Never leaves your Mac, deleted after 7 days Sent for transcription, then deleted
Summary A model on your Mac, or a provider you choose AI providers such as OpenAI and Anthropic
Notes Markdown files in your Obsidian vault Granola’s cloud, on AWS in the United States, with no EU data residency
Devices Mac with Apple Silicon Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android
Price Free and open source Free with 30 days of history; Business $14 and Enterprise $35 per user per month

Which one fits you

Granola, if you want

Notes on your phone or on Windows, a team that shares meetings in one place, integrations with Notion or HubSpot, and nothing to set up.

Takku, if you want

The audio to stay on your Mac, notes as files in your own vault, the freedom to choose or run the model that writes the summary, and no account or subscription.

Takku is for people and companies that can’t, or would rather not, send their meetings to a cloud in another country. Where your data goes lists everything it keeps and sends.
