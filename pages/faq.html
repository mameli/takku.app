Questions

Short answers to what people ask before they try Takku.

Is it free?
Yes, and open source under the MIT license. If the summary is written by a paid provider, you pay the provider directly. A model on your Mac costs nothing. For summaries with nothing to set up there is Takku Credit, prepaid, optional, about one cent per meeting. If Takku is useful to you, you can buy me a coffee: it helps cover the costs of keeping Takku going.
Does a bot join my call?
No. Takku records on your Mac, from your microphone and from what your Mac plays, so nobody in the call sees it. That is why you should tell them you’re recording.
Which call apps does it work with?
Any: Meet, Zoom, Teams, Slack, a call in the browser. Takku records everything your Mac plays, not one app, so pause music and videos during a meeting.
Does it work with headphones?
Yes, with headphones, AirPods or the Mac’s own speakers. Echo cancellation keeps the others’ voices out of your microphone.
Do I need Obsidian?
Takku writes Markdown files into a folder you choose. Obsidian is the nicest way to read them, with links between the note and the transcript, but any folder works.
Which languages does it understand?
Italian, English and 23 other European languages, one language per meeting. The summary can be written in any language.
Does it work offline?
The transcription does, once the model has been downloaded. The summary does too, if the model runs on your Mac.
How long until the summary is ready?
Takku transcribes the call in pieces while it goes on, so when you stop only the last few minutes are left. Then the summary takes as long as your model needs, and a notification tells you when it’s there.
Does the summary provider get my audio?
Never. It receives the transcript and your notes. Where your data goes lists everything that leaves your Mac.
How is it different from Granola?
Takku transcribes on your Mac, writes into your own vault instead of an online account, and lets you choose who writes the summary. There is no account and no subscription. Takku and Granola compares them point by point.
Can I use it on a Mac managed by my company?
Yes, if IT allows apps from identified developers: Takku is notarized by Apple. Some companies allow only the apps they approve: then ask IT. Check your company’s rules on recording meetings and on summary providers too.
Does it run on an Intel Mac?
No. Takku needs Apple Silicon and macOS 15 or later.
The summary gets a name wrong. What can I do?
Add the word to your vocabulary, or tell Takku who each speaker is, then press Retry. The guide shows how.
